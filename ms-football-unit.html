MIDDLE SCHOOL FLAG FOOTBALL
DAY 1 OF 9
Introduction & Throwing Mechanics
Learning Targets (SWBAT)
• I can correctly put on and wear a flag belt
• I can explain at least 3 safety rules for flag football
• I can demonstrate the throwing grip and release using the three cues
Equipment
• Flag belts (1 per student)
• Footballs (1 per pair)
• Cones · Pinnies
Standards
19.A19.B21.A
S1S4
Essential Question
What makes flag football different from tackle football — and how does that change how we play defense?  |  Why does proper throwing mechanics matter more than just throwing hard?
Warm-Up — Flag Tag  (5–8 min)

Students each get a flag belt. Everyone is "it" — pull anyone's flag, drop it on the ground (person picks it up and reattaches). Count how many flags you pull. No running outside boundaries. No contact. No grabbing clothing.

Play 2 rounds (90 seconds each). Between rounds: "How did you protect your flags? Did you turn your hips? Cut? Use your arms?" Brief discussion, then round 2.

This is the first time most students are wearing flag belts — let them figure out the feel before you teach anything.

Lesson Focus — Throwing Mechanics  (20–25 min)

Skill Cues — teach one, practice, then add the next:

Squish the Bug — Back foot rotates on the ball of the foot as you throw. Demonstrate without it (push throw, no power), then with it. "Your back foot is loading the gun. No pivot = no power."
Wipe the Windshield — Wrist snaps all the way through the throw, index finger last off the ball. Throwing hand finishes like wiping a windshield across the body. This puts spiral on the ball.
Flick the Booger — Index finger is the last thing to touch the ball and flicks off the tip. That's what makes the spiral spin tight.

Have students air-throw through all 3 cues before picking up a football. Then partner throw — 10–15 yards apart. Coach from center; if mechanics break down, stop the group and re-demo the one cue they're missing.

Catching Cues (add at 8-min mark):

Diamond Hands — Ball chest-high or above: thumbs + index fingers form a diamond. Catch in front of your face. Don't swipe — let it come to your hands.
Pinkies Together / Basket — Ball below the chest: pinkies come together and form a basket. Scoop it in.

Have pairs throw 10 passes each way. Count completions. No moving yet.

Game — Pin Football  (10–15 min)

Two teams. Each team has a line of pins (bowling pins, cones, or poly spots) on their side. Teams throw footballs to knock down opponent's pins while protecting their own. No running with the ball.

Round 1 only today — one pin per team. Students are learning the game AND the flag belt AND the throwing cues. Don't rush to multiple pins yet.

Closure & Assessment  (3–5 min)
Closure
Students sit with partner. "What are the three throwing cues? Say them to your partner." "What's one rule from today you didn't know before?" Preview: next class we level up throwing AND add catching on the move.
Assessment
Observe partner throw — are students applying all three throwing cues? Exit question: Can students name all three throwing cues unprompted? Observe Flag Tag — are students using hips/cuts to protect flags?
Differentiation & Teacher Notes
Modify down: Smaller ball or foam football for grip issues; step closer during partner throw.  Modify up: Increase partner distance; add a target (hoop, cone) to throw through.
Note: Flag belts always go on BEFORE any activity begins — build the habit from Day 1. Common mistake: kids holding the ball with a full-hand grip like a baseball. Get their fingers on the seams early. Non-judgment, skill-focused tone matters from the start.
MIDDLE SCHOOL FLAG FOOTBALL
DAY 2 OF 9
Throwing, Catching & REEF
Learning Targets (SWBAT)
• I can use all three throwing cues consistently when passing to a partner
• I can use the correct hand position (Diamond or Basket) based on where the ball is going
• I can work with my team to knock down opponent's pins
Equipment
• Flag belts (1 per student)
• Footballs (1 per pair for warm-up; 1 per group for Pin Football)
• Bowling pins / tall cones · Cones for boundaries
Standards
19.A19.B21.B
S1S2
Essential Question
How do Diamond Hands vs. the Basket change based on where the ball is thrown?  |  What does a good spiral look like and why does it matter for accuracy?
Warm-Up — Two-Line Passing  (5–8 min)

Two lines facing each other, 10–15 yards apart. Line A throws to Line B. Receiver catches, steps to the back of Line B. Thrower jogs to back of Line A. Continuous flow.

Coaching cues: "Squish the bug — I want to see that back foot pivot!"  ·  "Wrist through — Wipe the Windshield!"  ·  "Diamond hands if it's coming at your face — show me the diamond before it gets there!"

Run 4–5 minutes. This warm-up runs every class for the rest of the unit.

Lesson Focus — Moving Catch & REEF  (20–25 min)

Introduce REEF (ball carrying):

R — Ribs: Ball pressed tight against your ribs
E — Elbow: Elbow clamped down on the ball from above
E — Eagle Claw: Fingers spread wide, gripping the tip of the ball
F — Forearm: Forearm runs under the length of the ball

Demo it. Have every student hold an imaginary ball in REEF position for 10 seconds. Call a few up to demo. "When you're running with the ball, you protect it with REEF. If someone reaches for your flag, don't drop the ball — protect the ball, let them pull the flag."

Partner Throw and Catch — Moving Catch: Same pairs as Day 1, but now the receiver takes 3 steps sideways before the throw, catches on the move. Rotate roles every 5 catches. Coach: "Throw to where they're GOING, not where they ARE." After 8 minutes: increase to 5 steps diagonally.

Game — Pin Football All Rounds  (10–15 min)

Same field setup as Day 1. Run all three rounds today with full time each.

Round 1: 1 pin per team (review)  ·  Round 2: 2 pins per team (harder to defend)  ·  Round 3: 3 pins per team

Teams choose where to set their pins. Defense strategy becomes real. Let them figure it out. No running with the ball. Flag belts stay on.

Closure & Assessment  (3–5 min)
Closure
Students find a partner from the opposing team and share one answer each: "What's one thing your team did well on defense?" "What's one catching or throwing thing you're going to fix next class?" Teacher: "Two-Line Passing is our warm-up from now on. Every class starts there."
Assessment
Observe Two-Line Passing — are students leading the receiver? Check REEF position during drill — are students tucking the ball after the catch? Pin Football — are teams making decisions about where to place and defend pins?
Differentiation & Teacher Notes
Modify down: Focus on thumb-under-ball grip and wrist snap only — don't pile on all 3 cues at once for struggling throwers. Adjust pin placement distance closer for struggling teams.  Modify up: Increase distance in Two-Line Passing; have advanced throwers throw on the run.
Note: Two-Line Passing is your permanent warm-up — establish the routine hard this week so it runs itself. REEF is easy to teach, hard to remember under pressure — remind them again in Day 4 and Day 6.
MIDDLE SCHOOL FLAG FOOTBALL
DAY 3 OF 9
Route Running & Flickerball
Learning Targets (SWBAT)
• I can run a called route (fly, curl, out) with correct timing and separation
• I can throw a lead pass to a moving receiver
• I can explain and apply the basic rules of Flickerball
Equipment
• Flag belts (1 per student)
• Footballs (1 per group of 3)
• Cones for route markers & Flickerball boundaries
• Basketballs (optional for hoops)
Standards
19.B19.C
S1S2
Essential Question
Why does a receiver need to separate from the defender before the throw?  |  How does running a route help the quarterback know where to throw before the receiver gets there?
Warm-Up — Tick Tick Boom  (5–8 min)

Groups of 8–10 in a circle, one football. Students pass the football around the circle — any order, focus on throwing and catching form. One student kneels in the center next to a cone and continuously tries to flip it upright.

When the cone lands upright — BOOM. Whoever has the football at that moment takes a lap or performs a quick exercise (5 burpees, 10 jumping jacks — teacher's call). Once done, that student goes to the middle to flip the cone, the previous flipper joins the circle, and the game continues.

Keep the ball moving — no holding. Coaching cue: "Diamond hands or basket — read where it's coming." This is throwing and catching reps disguised as a game.

Lesson Focus — Route Running  (20–25 min)

Teach three routes using cones as markers. Walk first, then half-speed, then full speed.

Fly Route — Run straight downfield at full speed. No cut. QB throws deep.
Curl Route — Run 8–10 steps downfield, plant foot hard, spin back toward the QB. QB throws as receiver turns.
Out Route — Run 6–8 steps straight, plant hard, break 90 degrees to the sideline. QB leads to the outside.
Plant — Break — Look — Universal cue for ALL routes: every route ends with a hard plant, a cut, and eyes finding the ball.

Route Running — Groups of 3: QB, WR, cone line. QB calls the route. WR runs it. QB throws the lead pass. Rotate positions after every 3 routes. Run all three routes each rotation.

Coach: QB — throw to where they're GOING. WR — plant is the most important part of the route. Everyone tracks the ball with Diamond Hands or Basket based on height.

After 12 minutes: let each group self-select their favorite route and run it 5 times in a row at full speed. Track completions within the group.

Game — Flickerball  (10–15 min)

Basketball court or large open space. Two teams. No moving with the ball — feet must be planted when throwing. No guarding the ball carrier. 5 different teammates must touch the ball before a shot on goal.

Scoring: Shooter's Square = 1 pt  ·  Basket = 2 pts  ·  3-point line = 3 pts

Let them try it once with minimal coaching, then pause at the 5-minute mark: "What strategy is your team using? Are you spacing out or bunching up?" Let them adjust and resume.

Closure & Assessment  (3–5 min)
Closure
Students sit by position group (QBs together, WRs together). "Which route is hardest to throw to? Why?" "Which is hardest to run? Why?" "What does the QB need to know before the receiver cuts?" Connect to real game: routes exist to create separation.
Assessment
Route running drill — are WRs planting hard before their cut? QB decision-making — throwing to where the receiver is going, or where they were? Flickerball — are teams completing the 5-touch sequence, or skipping to the shot?
Differentiation & Teacher Notes
Modify down: Slow the cut to walking pace, then add speed. Have QBs who throw late call out "now" at the moment to release — verbal trigger helps timing. Allow more than 5 touches in Flickerball for struggling teams.  Modify up: Add a passive defender in route running groups.
Note: Groups of 3 is the sweet spot — no standing around. Watch for QBs who wait until the receiver stops moving before throwing — timing is the whole lesson today. Flickerball will feel chaotic at first — let it run a few minutes before coaching strategy.
MIDDLE SCHOOL FLAG FOOTBALL
DAY 4 OF 9
QB / WR / DB Rotation — Defense Introduced
Learning Targets (SWBAT)
• I can snap the ball and execute a throw after a QB drop
• I can run a called route against live defense and work to get open
• I can play defensive back and use pursuit angle to get to the ball carrier
Equipment
• Flag belts — flags active
• Footballs (1 per group of 3)
• Cones for routes & boundaries
• Pinnies for DB ID (optional)
Standards
19.B19.C
S2S4
Essential Question
How does playing defense change your understanding of offense?  |  What does "attack the hip, not the flag" mean — and why does it work?
Warm-Up — Two-Line Passing  (5–8 min)

Standard Two-Line Passing with coaching cue: "If you're catching, MOVE before the throw — shuffle two steps, then catch. Throwers, lead them." 4–5 minutes. This skill gets applied in the main activity today.

Lesson Focus — QB + WR + DB Rotation  (20–25 min)

Teach defense first:

Attack the Hip, Not the Flag — If you chase the flag, you're always behind the ball carrier. Identify which hip the flag is on, attack the hip, and the flag is right there. Demo: chase the flag → miss. Attack the hip → pull.
3-Step QB Drop — QB holds ball, takes 3 steps back to create throwing distance. Self-snap (QB holds and drops) keeps the drill running without needing a center.
DB Stance — Face the ball carrier, stay on the balls of your feet, weight forward. Don't reach until close enough to pull cleanly.

QB + WR + DB Rotation Drill: Groups of 3 — QB at line, WR at line, DB lined up 3 yards off the WR. QB calls the route → 3-step drop → WR runs route → DB defends → QB throws. Completion + no flag pulled = offense wins. Flag pulled = defense wins. Rotate positions every 3 plays. Each student plays all three positions.

Final 5 minutes: compete — track offense wins vs. defense wins per group. Groups report out.

Game — Ultimate Football  (10–15 min)

Large rectangular field with two end zones. Two teams. No running with the ball — 3 steps maximum after a catch. Complete a pass in the end zone to score. Defense wins ball on interceptions or incompletions. No contact.

Ultimate Football is the bridge game between route running and Mini Flag Football. It requires reading coverage, running routes, and defending passes — without the complexity of downs yet.

Closure & Assessment  (3–5 min)
Closure
Each group reports: "How many plays did offense win? How many did defense?" "What did defense do that made it hard to get open?" "Did knowing the defense's job help you play better offense?" Teacher: "Today you played every single position — the best players understand what the other side is doing."
Assessment
Rotation compliance — did every student play all three positions? DB technique — attacking the hip or chasing the flag? QB decision-making — throwing away from coverage?
Differentiation & Teacher Notes
Modify down: Shorten route to 5 yards for DBs struggling to pull flags. Have QBs call Fly only until mechanics are there. Let WRs run without a defender for 1 rotation to reset confidence.  Modify up: QB must throw within 3 seconds after the drop.
Note: This is the most important teaching day for game-readiness. Enforce rotation strictly — don't let dominant throwers hog QB. Watch for DBs who grab clothing — flag pulling only, call it immediately.
MIDDLE SCHOOL FLAG FOOTBALL
DAY 5 OF 9
Handoffs, Gauntlet & Flag Pull Top Gun
Learning Targets (SWBAT)
• I can execute a clean handoff using Football Hot Potato
• I can pull flags under pressure in the Gauntlet drill
• I can apply REEF when carrying the ball through defenders
Equipment
• Flag belts — flags active
• Footballs (1 per group for hot potato; 1–2 per gauntlet lane)
• Cones for Gauntlet lanes · Pinnies (optional)
Standards
19.A19.C21.B
S1S2
Essential Question
Why is a clean handoff important when the defense is reading the backfield?  |  What's the hardest part about pulling flags — and how do you get better at it?
Warm-Up — Two-Line Passing  (5–8 min)

Standard Two-Line Passing. Coaching cue: "After you catch it, tuck it into REEF before you jog to the back of the line. REEF every time. Build the habit." Look for students who catch and swing the ball around loosely — redirect to REEF immediately.

Lesson Focus — Handoffs + Gauntlet  (20–25 min)

Handoff Mechanics:

Giving: Extend ball with both hands at belly-button height. Place it firmly — don't toss, don't flip, PLACE it. Eyes forward, not on the exchange.
Receiving: Inside arm up at chest level, outside arm below — form a shelf (pocket). Feel the ball land in the shelf → immediately apply REEF. Don't grab — let it come to you, then secure.

Demo it wrong first (toss/flip), then right (firm placement into pocket). Students can see the difference immediately.

Drill 1 — Football Hot Potato (10 min): Groups of 5–6 in a circle, one football. Pass around using handoff technique — no throwing. Speed builds as they get comfortable. Drop = 3 jumping jacks, continue. After 4 minutes: mix in one throw every 3 handoffs — call "Pass!" or "Hand!" Final 2 minutes: count total handoffs without a drop in 60 seconds.

Drill 2 — Flag Pull Gauntlet (10 min): 2–3 lanes, 15 yards long. Ball carrier runs the lane. 3–4 defenders spaced along the lane — each gets ONE attempt to pull the ball carrier's flag as they pass. Ball carrier goal: get through with both flags. Defender goal: pull one flag cleanly. Run 5 min, then add a required cut — defenders adjust pursuit angle. Run another 5 min.

Attack the Hip, Not the Flag — Coach defenders: "If you go for the flag and miss, you're done. If you attack the hip, you're always in position."
Game — Flag Pull Top Gun  (10–15 min)

1v1 flag pulling contest. Pair students by size/athletic level (teacher discretion). Both players have flag belts. Start 5 yards apart. On "Go!" — both try to pull the other's flag. First pull wins. Winner stays, loser rotates. Tournament bracket style — 2–3 rounds.

Keep energy high — call out great pulls, great cuts, clean escapes. This is a competitive drill with high buy-in.

Closure & Assessment  (3–5 min)
Closure
Students pair up: "One thing that helped you survive the Gauntlet." "One thing that made it easy to pull flags in Top Gun." Teacher: "Defense in flag football is not about being faster. It's about angles. You attack the hip, you always have a chance — even against the fastest kid in the class."
Assessment
Handoff technique — are students creating the pocket and placing the ball, or tossing/grabbing? REEF under pressure — are ball carriers tucking the ball during the Gauntlet? Top Gun — using hip-attacking angles or just lunging at the flags?
Differentiation & Teacher Notes
Modify down: Two-handed exchanges at slower speed before building to full speed for Hot Potato. Reduce Gauntlet to 2 defenders for early success. Pair Top Gun thoughtfully — avoid mismatches.  Modify up: Full-speed Gauntlet with cuts in both directions.
Note: Hot Potato = highest reps, lowest threat in the unit. The Gauntlet creates natural urgency. Top Gun will be the most popular activity of the unit — use it as an earned reward in future classes if needed. Watch for ball carriers who stop and spin — keep them moving forward.
MIDDLE SCHOOL FLAG FOOTBALL
DAY 6 OF 9
Punting & Agility
Learning Targets (SWBAT)
• I can execute a stationary drop punt using correct mechanics
• I can catch a punted ball using the correct hand position
• I can communicate with a partner to call for and catch punts safely
Equipment
• Flag belts (1 per student)
• Footballs (1 per pair)
• Cones for agility circuit & boundaries
• Poly spots for punt zones
Standards
19.A19.B21.B
S1S3
Essential Question
When would a team choose to punt in flag football?  |  Why does dropping the ball flat (parallel to the ground) matter for a clean punt?
Warm-Up — Football Agility Circuit  (5–8 min)

Set up 3–4 cone stations with 8–10 students rotating through. 20 seconds per station, rotate on whistle. Two full rotations.

Station 1 — Lateral Shuffle: Shuffle between two cones 10 yards apart, touch cone, change direction.  Station 2 — Backpedal to Sprint: Backpedal 5 yards, plant, sprint forward 10 yards.  Station 3 — Two-Line Passing Mini: 8 yards apart, throw-and-go.  Station 4 — Cone Cuts: Sprint to a cone, plant hard, cut left or right, sprint out.

Lesson Focus — Punting  (20–25 min)

Four-Step Punt Sequence — teach each step separately, then combine:

Step and Drop — Take a small step forward with your kicking-side foot. Drop the ball — don't toss it up. Drop it.
Drop it Flat — Ball held parallel to the ground, nose slightly angled down. When you drop it, it falls in a flat spin. End-over-end punts go nowhere.
Lock the Ankle — Point your toe down, lock your ankle. Kick with the top of your foot, not your toe. A floppy foot kills the punt.
Follow Through — Kicking leg follows straight through and up toward your shoulder.

Have all students air-kick through the sequence twice before picking up a football. Common mistake: students hold the ball with one hand and swing it as they kick — force two-hand hold and deliberate drop.

Partner Punt and Catch — 3 Rounds: Pairs, 15–20 yards apart. Round 1 (5 min): stationary punt, technique focus. Round 2 (5 min): catcher calls "I got it!" before it lands — communication drill. Round 3 (5 min): increase to 20–25 yards, catcher tracks ball from the kick. Challenge: 3 punts in a row without a drop.

Game — Ultimate Football with Punt Variation  (10–15 min)

Standard Ultimate Football rules: no running with ball (3 steps max), score in end zone by completed pass, defense takes over on drops or interceptions, no contact.

Punt variation: If your team is 4+ steps from the end zone and wants to flip field position, a player can call "PUNT!" and punt downfield. Opposing team catches or retrieves it. Keeps the punt skill active in a game context.

Closure & Assessment  (3–5 min)
Closure
Students rate their punt on a 1–3 scale with fingers: 1 = "I'm still figuring out the drop." 2 = "I get the mechanics, needs more reps." 3 = "I feel consistent." Teacher: "The drop is the whole skill. Everything else follows from a clean drop."
Assessment
Observe partner punt — flat drop or end-over-end? Self-assessment scale — are students accurately rating their own skill level? Game — did any teams use the punt play variation? Were they successful?
Differentiation & Teacher Notes
Modify down: Place a cone on the ground, have students drop the ball to the top of it repeatedly — builds muscle memory before adding the kick. Reduce distance to 10 yards.  Modify up: Challenge for hang time — high punts over distance.
Note: Punting is the hardest skill in the unit for middle schoolers — most will have zero experience. The flat drop is the single most important coaching point. If class time is short, cut the agility circuit to 2 stations and keep the full punt lesson.

DAY 7 OF 9
Receivers vs. Defenders — Reading the Route
Learning Targets (SWBAT)
• I can use route running to create separation from a defender
• I can play defensive back position, reading the receiver's hips and hands
• I can apply all throwing, catching, and carrying cues in a live game situation
Equipment
• Flag belts — flags active
• Footballs (1 per group)
• Cones for field setup & zones
• Pinnies (2 colors)
Standards
19.C21.A
S2S4
Essential Question
What does a receiver do after they make the catch that changes what the defense does next?  |  How do you read the receiver's body language as a defender?
Warm-Up — Two-Line Passing Competitive  (5–8 min)

Two-Line Passing with competitive scoring. Line A vs. Line B — track completions for 3 minutes, switch direction and track again. Winning line picks first group activity position.

Coaching cue: "Throwers — call your receiver's name before you throw. WRs — show your hands early, give the QB a target."

Lesson Focus — Receivers vs. Defenders  (20–25 min)

Teach defensive reading:

Read the Hips, Not the Eyes — Wherever the hips go, the body goes. Eyes and head fake. Hips don't lie. Demo: QB throws a Curl — show the DB following hips vs. biting on head fake. The student who follows hips defends it.
Watch the Hands — When the receiver shows their hands, the ball is coming. DB closes on the ball immediately.

Receivers vs. Defenders Drill: Groups of 4 — 1 QB, 2 WRs, 2 DBs (or 1 QB, 1 WR, 1 DB for smaller class). Two route trees run simultaneously (left side, right side). QB snaps and reads — throws to whichever WR has separation. DBs cover their WR, attack the hip on the catch.

Scoring: Offense point = completion + WR keeps both flags for 3 steps after catch. Defense point = flag pull, interception, or incompletion. Rotate positions every 4 plays. Run for the full 20 minutes. Final 5 minutes: "Offense vs. Defense Challenge" — each group goes 5 plays, tracks score.

Game — Ultimate Football Full Rules  (10–15 min)

Two teams, full field. Standard Ultimate Football: no running (3 steps max), score in end zone by completed pass, defense takes over on drops or interceptions, no contact.

Today's emphasis: "Use what you learned in the drill. Receivers — run real routes, not just random zigzags. Defense — read hips, attack hip on the catch."

Closure & Assessment  (3–5 min)
Closure
Students reflect 30 seconds, then share with a partner: "One offensive thing you did well today." "One thing defense did that you couldn't beat." Class share-out: build a board of "what works" for offense and defense — students carry this into Mini Flag Football.
Assessment
DB technique — reading hips or biting on head fakes? QB decision-making — choosing the open receiver based on coverage, or defaulting to the same target? Group score tracking — which groups had balanced offense vs. defense scores?
Differentiation & Teacher Notes
Modify down: Reduce to Fly route only for DBs getting beat. Slow drill to half-speed for WRs who don't get open. Simplify to 1 WR / 1 DB for QBs struggling to read two routes.  Modify up: Add a 3-second throw clock for QBs.
Note: This is the last skill-building day before Mini Flag Football — treat it as review and refinement. Watch for students avoiding QB in rotations — coach them up specifically today. "Read the hips, not the eyes" will stick. Students will quote it back in the game.
MIDDLE SCHOOL FLAG FOOTBALL
DAY 8 OF 9
Mini Flag Football
Learning Targets (SWBAT)
• I can apply offensive and defensive strategies in a flag football game
• I can follow flag football rules including 4-down structure and flag pulling
• I can work cooperatively with my team to move the ball and score
Equipment
• Flag belts — flags active
• Footballs (1 per field)
• Cones for mini fields & end zones
• Pinnies (multiple colors) · Footballs for warm-up rotation
Standards
19.C21.A21.B
S2S4
Essential Question
How do you call a play when you only have 10 seconds before the snap?  |  What does your team do differently on 4th down vs. 1st down?
Warm-Up — QB / WR / DB Rotation Review  (5–8 min)

Quick review of the Day 4 rotation drill. Groups of 3 — QB, WR, DB. QB calls a route (Fly, Curl, or Out), WR runs it, DB defends. QB throws. Rotate positions every 2 plays. Keep it fast — this is warm-up pace, not teaching pace. Students already know the drill.

Coaching cue: "Read the hips, not the eyes. Attack the hip, not the flag. You've got 5 minutes — use them." This gets every student mentally into QB/WR/DB roles before Mini Flag Football starts.

Lesson Focus — Mini Flag Football Rules & 5 Passes  (20–25 min)

Walk through Mini Flag Football rules before playing:

10-Second Huddle — Teams get 10 seconds before each play. QB calls the play: name the route and the receiver. "Jordan, run a curl. Alex, run a fly. I'm throwing to whoever's open."
4-Down Structure — 4 downs to score (reach opponent's end zone). No score in 4 downs: possession switches.
5-Count Rush Rule — No rushing the QB until 5 seconds after the snap.
QB Cannot Run — QB must pass or hand off on every play. No contact, no blocking.

Walk through one "practice play" with a demo group before games start.

5 Passes Warm-Up Game (5–6 min): Teams must complete 5 consecutive passes to score a point. Knocked down or intercepted = possession switches. No running with the ball. Count each complete pass aloud as a team. Warms up decision-making and spacing without the complexity of downs.

Mini Flag Football (15–20 min): Groups of 6, multiple mini fields. Teacher role: rotate between fields, enforce 5-count rush rule, call obvious flags. Encourage huddles — if teams aren't huddling, stop the game and make them. "You have 10 seconds. Use them." At 10-min mark: stop all games, give one whole-class coaching point on the most common mistake seen, then resume.

Closure & Assessment  (3–5 min)
Closure
Teams stay together, self-evaluate: "What play worked best for your offense? Why?" "What did defense do to stop the other team?" "What's one thing your team needs to fix for the tournament?" Teacher: "Tomorrow is the tournament. You've built these skills across 8 days — now put it all together."
Assessment
Huddle compliance — are teams huddling and calling plays, or just snapping randomly? 5-count rush — are defenders counting before rushing? 5 Passes — are teams spreading the floor and creating passing lanes, or bunching?
Differentiation & Teacher Notes
Modify down: Reduce to 3v3, smaller field, eliminate the 4-down structure and just play keep-away first.  Modify up: Add a running play option for one designated player who isn't the QB.
Note: Zombie Tag burns energy fast, students love it, and requires zero football skill — great equalizer before competition. The 10-second huddle rule is the biggest game-changer — enforce it hard today so Day 9 runs itself. Common issue: students who played tackle football outside school will try option routes or blocking assignments — keep it simple and redirect.

DAY 9 OF 9
Tournament Day
Learning Targets (SWBAT)
• I can compete in a flag football tournament applying skills from the full unit
• I can demonstrate sportsmanship and teamwork throughout competition
• I can self-evaluate my skill growth from Day 1 to Day 9

Essential Question
What does sportsmanship look like in a competitive game?  |  How have your skills changed from when you started this unit?
Warm-Up — Two-Line Passing Final Reps  (5–8 min)

Last time running Two-Line Passing as a class. Students know the drill. Run it with intensity — competitive tracking. Line A vs. Line B, count completions in 3 minutes.

"Last warm-up of the unit. These are your final reps before the tournament. Make them count."

Tournament Setup & Games  (25–30 min)

Teams of 4–5 players. Option A — students count off by number, teacher controls size, 60 seconds to choose a team name. Option B — teacher pre-assigns balanced teams based on skill level. Write team names on board. Set up bracket (round robin if 4 teams; single elimination if 6+).

Tournament Rules: Each game — 10 minutes, continuous play. 4 downs to score. Flag pulling. 5-count rush. QB cannot run. Teams keep their own score, teacher verifies. Report score, rotate to next opponent.

Run 2–3 round-robin games per team. Teacher rotates between fields — be visible, call obvious violations, give live coaching feedback. Between games (90-sec transition): teams huddle, teacher gives one whole-class positive note, teams adjust and rotate.

Sportsmanship Standard (non-negotiable): Before each game — both teams shake hands or fist bump. After each game — both teams shake hands AND say one positive thing about the opposing team out loud before walking away. Teacher enforces without exception.

Championship Game (if time allows): Top 2 teams from round robin. Class gathers at one field to watch. Same game, same standards. Spectators cheer for both teams.

Unit Reflection & Closure  (8–10 min)
Reflection Questions
1. "What skill improved the most for you from Day 1 to today?" (Throwing, catching, route running, defense, pulling flags — pick one and explain.)
2. "Name one thing you learned about being a teammate or competitor in this unit."
3. "What rule or cue from this unit will you remember 5 years from now? Say it out loud."

After individual reflection: open share-out. Collect cue answers on the board — students will almost always say REEF, Squish the Bug, attack the hip. Validate these.
Assessment + Teacher Close
Tournament — applying skills from Days 1–8 in live game? Sportsmanship — completing handshake + positive comment without prompting? Reflection quality — specific about skill growth, or vague?

"Flag football is not about being the fastest or the strongest. It's about knowing your job, trusting your teammates, and making smart decisions under pressure. You just spent 9 days building that. That transfers — not just to football, but to every team situation you'll ever be in. Nice work."
Differentiation & Teacher Notes
Skill balancing: If one team dominates, add a rule mid-tournament (e.g., every player must touch the ball before a score — forces everyone in).  Limited mobility: Allow permanent QB role or "coach on the field" if full participation isn't possible.  High-competition students: Address sportsmanship privately before the tournament, not publicly during.
Note: Pre-assign teams the night before — Day 9 transitions take time and student drama over teams slows everything down. Post the bracket where students can see it — even a rough whiteboard bracket creates investment. The written reflection closes the unit with intentionality — don't skip it even if you're running short on time. One question is better than zero.

Catching
Diamond Hands · Pinkies Together · REEF
I use the right hand shape based on where the ball is coming, and I tuck into REEF after every catch.
Route Running
Fly · Curl · Out · Plant — Break — Look
I run my routes with a hard plant before every cut, and I find the ball with my eyes immediately after the break.
Defense — Flag Pulling
Attack the Hip, Not the Flag · Read the Hips, Not the Eyes
I attack the ball carrier's hip instead of chasing the flag, and I read the receiver's hips to avoid getting faked out.
Sportsmanship & Teamwork
Encourage teammates · Play fair · Handle losing/winning well
I encouraged my teammates, played by the rules, and treated the other team with respect — win or lose.
