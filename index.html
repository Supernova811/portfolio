Case Study: Confidence Through Structured Goal Setting

The Challenge

Middle school PE students often disengage when they perceive themselves as "not athletic." Research in sport psychology (specifically Bandura's Self-Efficacy Theory and Locke & Latham's Goal-Setting Theory) suggests that confidence is built through structured success experiences, not innate talent. How do you design a learning experience that teaches students to build their own confidence?

Design Process

Analysis

Learner Profile: Grades 6-8, mixed ability levels, many with low physical self-concept. Students often set vague goals ("get better") or avoid challenge entirely.

Needs Assessment: Students lacked a framework for translating effort into progress. Without measurable targets, they couldn't recognize improvement, leading to disengagement.

Standards Alignment: SHAPE America Standards 4 & 5, CASEL SEL Competencies (Self-Awareness, Self-Management), Illinois State Goals 19, 20, 21.

Design Decisions

  • Scaffolded 5-lesson structure: each lesson builds on the previous, moving from understanding confidence to applying it
  • Interactive format: knowledge checks, reflection prompts, slider activities, scenario-based learning, and a goal-building tool
  • Grounded in research: Bandura's four sources of self-efficacy, Locke & Latham's goal-setting principles
  • Accessible and responsive: WCAG 2.1 AA compliance, works on any device
  • Certificate of completion with a personal Confidence Plan students can print and use

Development

Built as a single-file interactive web application using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. No external dependencies or subscriptions required. Progress tracking via localStorage. Rise-quality interactions without the $1,399/year price tag.

Results

A fully functional, standards-aligned interactive learning module ready for classroom deployment. Demonstrates proficiency in instructional design, learning science, interactive content development, and accessibility compliance.

Case Study: K-5 Volleyball Unit

The Challenge

Design a multi-week volleyball unit for K-5 students that progresses from zero experience to game play, differentiates across wide ability ranges, and aligns to Illinois State Learning Standards.

Design Process

D

Analysis

Learner Profile: K-5 students across multiple schools, ages 5-11, wide range of motor development and prior experience.

Standards: IL State Goal 19 (motor skills), Goal 20 (fitness), Goal 21 (teamwork and cooperation).

Design: Scaffolded Skill Progression

  • Week 1: Underhand serving, individual wall practice with specific cues (step, hit with heel of hand, follow through)
  • Week 2: Bumping/Forearm pass, individual drills progressing to partner passing with measurable outcomes
  • Week 3: Setting/Overhand pass, technique with hoop targets for accuracy measurement
  • Week 4: Overhand serving, advanced skill with biomechanical cues
  • Week 5: Modified game play, applying all skills in game situations

Key Instructional Strategies

  • Explicit cue-based instruction: every skill broken into 3-5 observable cues
  • Scaffolded complexity: individual practice → partner drills → small group → game
  • Differentiation: beach balls/trainers for developing students, regulation balls for advanced
  • Assessment: teacher observation against cue performance, student self-assessment, peer feedback
  • SEL integration: reflection prompts, encouragement protocols, cooperation standards
  • Bellringers: cognitive warm-ups connecting to lesson content

Results

Delivered across 3 schools, K-5, with documented student progression from individual skill practice to cooperative game play. Unit demonstrates backward design, scaffolded instruction, standards alignment, differentiation, and formative assessment — all core instructional design competencies.

Analysis

Learner Profile: K-8 students on school Chromebooks, wide range of bowling experience.

Needs: Real bowling scoring (not simplified), grade-appropriate content, engagement features, teacher oversight.

Constraints: Must work offline, no accounts, no installation, single HTML file, Chromebook-compatible.

Design Decisions

  • Real 10-frame scoring: strikes, spares, and 10th frame bonus rules — not a simplified version
  • Three game modes: Solo, Party (2-6 players pass-and-play), Team (A vs B)

  • Leaderboards: grade-based and school-wide with a Hall of Fame
  • Projectable awards page: end-of-unit class celebration screen
  • Hidden teacher dashboard: 5-tap access + PIN for player management and data reset
  • Dark arcade aesthetic: intentionally designed to appeal to students
  • K-2 printable score sheets: for younger students not using the app independently

Development

Single HTML file, vanilla JavaScript, no dependencies. localStorage for persistent data per device. SVG mascot characters with animated reactions. Responsive design optimized for Chromebooks. Deployed to Netlify.

Case Study: Middle School Volleyball Unit

The Challenge

Middle school students arrive with varying volleyball experience, shorter tolerance for isolated drill work, and a strong desire to compete and play. A K-5 scaffolding approach — one skill per week, game play reserved for the end — doesn't match where they are developmentally. How do you design a unit that meets MS students where they are while still building real skill?

Design Process

Analysis

Learner Profile: Grades 6-8 at Barbour School, mixed ability, diverse volleyball backgrounds. Students are cognitively ready to process multiple skills simultaneously. They want to play — extended drill work loses them fast.

Needs Assessment: Students need bump and set fundamentals, serving and receiving, and tactical game sense. They also need opportunities to lead — middle schoolers respond to responsibility and ownership.

Constraints: 7 lessons (~3-4 weeks), FitnessGram testing integrated, shared gym space. Design must maximize active time.

Design Decisions

  • Tactical games approach: game play introduced by Lesson 3 — students learn skills in context, not isolation
  • Multi-skill from Day 1: bump and set introduced together, reflecting MS motor readiness
  • Progressive student autonomy: peer counting (Lesson 2) → captains (Lesson 3) → student referees (Lesson 7)
  • FitnessGram integration: fitness testing embedded in Lessons 3 and 6 rather than taught as a separate unit
  • Sport-specific warm-ups: volleyball movement patterns (karaoke, back pedal, jog/sprint) rather than general fitness games
  • Consistent cue language: same three bump cues and three set cues repeated every lesson until automatic

Results

Students progressed from individual skill practice to self-managed game play in 7 lessons. By Lesson 7, students were officiating their own games, demonstrating rule comprehension and personal accountability that extended beyond physical skill.

Case Study: Volleyball Across Grade Bands — Developmental Progression Analysis

The Challenge

Any curriculum developer can design a lesson. The real skill is designing across developmental levels — understanding how the same content requires fundamentally different instructional choices depending on who the learner is. This analysis documents how volleyball curriculum changes from K-5 to grades 6-8, and more importantly, why.

What This Demonstrates

  • Pacing: One skill per week (K-5) vs. multi-skill from Day 1 (MS)
  • Equipment: Balloon-to-regulation ladder (K-5) vs. regulation as default (MS)
  • Game play timing: Final 2 weeks (K-5) vs. integrated by Lesson 3 (MS)
  • Student role: Learner (K-5) vs. player → referee → game leader (MS)
  • Assessment: Cue demonstration (K-5) vs. tactical application (MS)
  • Theoretical grounding: Vygotsky ZPD scaffolding (K-5) vs. Teaching Games for Understanding (MS)

Why It Matters for Curriculum Design

The standards are identical. The sport is identical. The teacher is identical. What changes is the learner — and good curriculum design responds to the learner, not just the content. This analysis documents those decisions explicitly, connecting each design choice to developmental theory and practical classroom reality.

Case Study: MS Flag Football — 9-Day Unit Plan

The Challenge

Middle school students arrive at a flag football unit with wildly different backgrounds — some have played organized football, many haven't touched a ball. A single-track lesson plan fails both groups. The challenge: design a 9-day unit that builds real skill from day one, keeps advanced students challenged, and ends with a competitive tournament every student is prepared to play in.

Design Process

Analysis

Learner Profile: Grades 6-8, mixed athletic backgrounds, wide range of football experience. Students are cognitively ready for multi-skill instruction but need structured entry points — especially students who've never held a football.

Needs Assessment: Students need throwing mechanics, catching technique, route running, defensive positioning, and game rules — built progressively so game play is meaningful, not chaotic, by Day 8.

Standards: IL Goals 19.A, 19.B, 21.A — SHAPE America Standards S1, S4, S5.

Design Decisions

  • Skill-first, game-integrated: isolated skill work on Day 1, applied in modified games by Day 3, full game by Day 8
  • 45+ explicit skill cues: every skill broken into named, memorable cues ("Squish the Bug," "Wipe the Windshield," "REEF" for catching) — repeatable language that transfers from drill to game
  • 9-day progression: Throwing → Catching → Routes → QB/WR/DB → Handoffs → Punting → Defense → Mini Game → Tournament
  • Differentiation on every lesson: modify-down and modify-up options built into each day — not an afterthought
  • SWBAT learning targets + essential questions: every lesson opens with "I can" statements and a driving question connecting skills to game IQ
  • School color customizer: teachers pick their own primary and accent colors before printing — every school gets a unit plan that looks like it was built for them
  • Print-ready PDF format: each day fits one 8.5×11 page, designed for binder use in a gym with no screen

Development

Built as a single HTML file with a live color picker UI that updates CSS variables in real time. Teachers select school colors, preview the result, and print — no design skills required. Standards badges include hover tooltips with full standard descriptions. Deployed to Teachers Pay Teachers.

Results

A complete, print-ready 9-day unit plan deployed as a commercial product on Teachers Pay Teachers. Demonstrates progressive curriculum design, scaffolded skill development, differentiation, standards alignment, and a teacher-facing UX feature (color customizer) that no competing product offers.
